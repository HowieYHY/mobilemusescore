# PocketScore

**Your MuseScore scores on your phone or tablet. Read them, play them, practise your part.**

### [Open PocketScore](https://howieyhy.github.io/mobilemusescore/)

- Opens the `.mscz` files you make in MuseScore.
- Plays them with the same sounds as MuseScore on your computer.
- Turn any part up, down, off or solo it. Slow it down, or loop a passage.
- Write on the score with your finger or a stylus.
- Free, no account, no ads, and it works offline.

---

## Get it (2 minutes)

No app store needed.

**iPad / iPhone**
1. Open the link above in **Safari** or **Chrome**.
2. Tap **Share**: in Safari it's under **•••** at the bottom (on iPad, at the top right); in Chrome it's beside the address.
3. Tap **View More** → **Add to Home Screen** (further down the list) → **Add**.
4. Open **PocketScore** from your home screen.

**Android**
1. Open the link above in **Chrome**.
2. Tap **Install PocketScore** → **Install**.
   No button? Tap Chrome's **⋮** menu → **Add to Home screen** → **Install**.
3. Open **PocketScore** from your home screen.

**Computer:** open the link in Chrome, Edge or Safari.

> The first score you open downloads the instrument sounds (about **50 MB**), so use Wi-Fi.
> After that, PocketScore works without internet.

## Good to know

- **Your notes and saved settings stay on this device only.** They are not in the `.mscz` file.
  People you share the score with won't see them, and you won't see them on your other devices.
- **Removing PocketScore deletes them.** So does clearing Safari's or Chrome's website data.
  There is no backup or export yet.
- **Nothing is uploaded.** Your scores are opened on your device and never sent anywhere.
  PocketScore keeps a short log of how playback went (times, score names, how the device kept up;
  never your notes or files), on this device too: it leaves only if you send it yourself (see
  *Playback report* in [Settings](#settings)).
  (The app itself is downloaded from GitHub's website hosting, which sees visits like any website.
  PocketScore has no accounts, ads or tracking.)
- **The music pauses when you leave the app** (another app, home screen, screen locked), and
  goes quiet at once. Tap Play when you're back. To keep it playing instead, turn on **Keep playing when I leave
  PocketScore** in **Settings** (the gear at the top).
- **It updates itself.** With internet, it checks for a new version when you open it, when you come
  back to it, and every 30 minutes. If a score is open, it restarts into the new version when you
  switch away, or after 20 seconds without a touch while the music is stopped. It never restarts
  while music plays or while the mixer, speed, loop or notes are open. Your score comes back where
  you were (page, view, zoom, speed and loop), with unsaved changes kept. The version number is on
  the start screen.
- **On a version older than 1.0.8?** Those don't update while a score is open. Once, with internet,
  close PocketScore completely (swipe it away in your recent apps, or close the browser tab) and open
  it again. If the start screen still shows the old number, wait a moment and do it once more. After
  that it updates by itself.

## How to…

**Not sure what a button does?** Tap **?** at the top for a one-minute tour of the controls.

### Open and play
| To… | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| Open a score | Tap **Open score**, choose a `.mscz` file |
| Open a score from another app (Android) | In **Files**, tap ⋮ beside the score, then **Share**, **PocketScore** |
| Play / pause | Tap **▶** / **❚❚** |
| Start from a note | Tap the note |
| Hear one note | While stopped, tap it |
| Jump around | Drag the position slider |
| Go to a bar | Tap the bar number under the slider, type the bar, and press **Go** (or Enter). Bars are numbered as printed on the score |
| Go to a section | Pick it from the menu beside the bar number (*Intro*, *Verse 1*, *Chorus*…). It only appears when the score has section marks (MuseScore's rehearsal marks) |
| Back to the start | Tap **⏮** |
| Zoom | Pinch, or use **−** and **+** |
| Scroll | Drag with one or two fingers |
| Move quickly through a long score | Start scrolling, then drag the handle that appears at the right edge (along the bottom in *Continuous (horizontal)*). It shows the page, or in the continuous views the time in the music, and fades away when you stop |
| Change the layout | Menu at the top right: *Page view*, *Page by page*, *Two pages* or *Continuous* |
| Read one page at a time | Choose *Page by page*: each page fills the screen whole; swipe left or right (or use the arrow keys) to turn one page. The page turns by itself as the music reaches the next one. You can zoom in (pinch, or **+**), but never out past the whole page; zoomed in, drag to move around the page, and keep swiping past its edge to turn. In notes mode, use two fingers |
| Read two pages side by side | Choose *Two pages*, with the phone or tablet on its side. The bars go away (Focus starts with this view; tap off the music to bring the controls back) and pages 1 and 2 sit together in the middle of the screen. It works like a camera over the pages laid in a row: swipe left and the view slides along one page, so the right page moves to the left with the next page beside it (2 and 3, then 3 and 4); swipe right to go back; pinch or use + and − to move the camera closer. On a wide screen, such as a phone on its side, part of the page before and the page after shows at the edges. The arrow keys turn too, and the pages move on by themselves as the music plays. To turn both pages at once instead (1-2, then 3-4), switch on **Two pages: a swipe turns both** in Settings. On Android the screen turns on its side for you where the phone allows it (the installed app, and Chrome once the view has gone full screen); on an iPhone or iPad, turn it yourself |
| Read with nothing in the way | Tap **⛶** (Focus) at the top |
| Change how PocketScore works | Tap the **gear** (Settings) at the top. See [Settings](#settings) |
| Close a panel | Tap **Done**, or your phone's **Back** |

A blue line shows where you are, and the page follows it.

<details>
<summary>More about opening scores</summary>

- **iPad and iPhone:** save your scores to the **Files** app first (AirDrop them, or save them
  from email or a cloud drive). **Open score** goes straight to Files.
- **Android:** the file list shows all your files, not only scores. Scores end in `.mscz`.
- **Android, from another app:** share the score to PocketScore (in **Files**: ⋮, **Share**, **PocketScore**)
  and it opens straight away. PocketScore is in the Share list once it is installed from Chrome. It isn't
  in *Open with*, which Android keeps for apps from the Play Store. From **Telegram**, save the file
  first (⋮, **Save to Downloads**) and share it from Files: Telegram's own Share finds no app for
  scores. Because Android doesn't recognise `.mscz` as a type, PocketScore also appears when sharing
  other unknown files; it says so if a file isn't a score.
- PocketScore opens `.mscz` and `.mscx` files. Not PDF, MusicXML or MIDI.
- Opening another score stops the music.
- If you scroll away while it plays, the page waits a moment before following the music again. To
  turn the page yourself instead, turn off **Follow the music** in Settings.
- **Focus** hides everything but the music and a play button. On Android, a computer or an iPad it also
  goes full screen (no browser bars); an iPhone's Safari doesn't allow that, so there it hides
  PocketScore's own bars. Tapping a note still plays from it. To bring the controls back, tap anywhere
  off the music (a margin, the title, the white space between staves or lines, around the page), or
  use your phone's **Back** or **Esc**.
- **Focus also starts by itself** when you leave the screen alone: after 4 seconds while the music
  plays, 10 seconds while it's stopped (both can be changed, or this turned off, in Settings). Not
  while a panel (mixer, speed, loop, settings) or notes are open. It hides PocketScore's bars but
  doesn't go full screen (browsers only allow that after your own tap).

</details>

### Practise: slower, or on repeat
Two round buttons next to **Mixer**: **speed** (the dial) and **loop** (the arrows).

| To… | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| Slow down / speed up | Tap the **speed** button, then **−** / **+** (5% steps) or the slider (10% to 300%) |
| Play at an exact tempo | In the same panel, type a number next to **♩ =** |
| Loop a passage | Tap the **loop** button. Tap **Set loop marker left**, then tap the passage's first note. Tap **Set loop marker right**, then tap its last note |
| Loop on / off | **Loop playback** (with no markers, the whole score loops) |
| Remove the loop | **Clear loop markers** |

- The markers show on the score as blue lines with a flag: **▶** where the loop starts, **◀** where it ends.
  A blue band under the position slider marks the loop too.
- **♩ =** shows the tempo where the music is now (in quarter notes per minute, as MuseScore shows it),
  with your speed applied. It follows tempo changes in the score as it plays.
- A marker button turns blue while it waits for your tap; tap it again to cancel. Tapping a note for
  a marker doesn't move the music or play the note.
- Once you change the speed, the speed button shows the tempo (for example **103**). The loop button
  turns blue while looping.
- Speed and loop are **not saved**: the next score you open starts at 100%, with no loop.
- On phones, the position slider sits on its own row above the buttons.

### Mixer: hear your part
Tap **Mixer**.

| To… | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| Make a part louder or softer | Its slider. **100%** = as saved in the score |
| Silence a part | **M** (mute) |
| Hear only some parts | **S** (solo), on one or more parts |
| Change a part's instrument | Tap the sound under its name (e.g. *Grand Piano*) |
| Change several parts at once | In that list, tap **Several parts** |
| Hear a click | Unmute **Metronome** at the bottom (it's muted every time a score opens, unless you turn on **Metronome on when a score opens** in Settings) |
| Room echo | Tap **Reverb** at the top |
| Overall volume | **Master** |

<details>
<summary>More about the mixer</summary>

- The mixer works as soon as the score is open; you don't need to press Play first.
- It starts with the volume, mute and solo settings saved in your score, as MuseScore does.
  If a score opens silent, its parts may have been saved muted.
- **50%** sounds about half as loud, **200%** about twice as loud.
- In the sound list, *In score* marks the sound the score uses, and *Each part's sound in the
  score* puts every part back.
- Mixer changes are kept when you tap **Save**.

</details>

### Write on the score
Tap the **pencil** at the top, then tap **Done** when finished.

| Tool | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Pen** / **Highlight** | Write or mark with a stylus or finger |
| **Text** | **Double-tap** the score to add a text box. Tap outside it when done |
| **Erase** | Tap or rub over a mark or text box |
| **Colours** | Black is the default. **+** for more |
| **Size** (dots) | How thick the pen, highlighter or eraser is |
| **Undo** / **Clear** | Clear removes everything on this score |

<details>
<summary>More about notes</summary>

- **Draw with finger:** when on, one finger draws and two fingers scroll. Turn it off to scroll with
  one finger and draw only with a stylus (then tapping a note with your finger plays it).
  It turns off by itself the first time you use a stylus; you can turn it back on.
- **Text boxes:** tap a box to change it, drag the arrows on its left to move it, drag its bottom
  corner to resize it (the text grows with it), tap **×** to delete it. While you're typing, a new
  colour changes that box.
- Notes follow the music: a note written in one view shows in the others on the same notes (Page view
  and Page by page show the same page). Marks away from the music, such as in a margin or by the
  title, stay in the view they were written in. A text box from another view can be erased here but
  is changed in the view it was written in. **Clear** removes your notes from every view.

</details>

### Start a new score

> **Not in the published app yet.** New score and Edit are still being tried out and are hidden at
> pocketscore's public address for now; adding `?composer` to the address shows them.

On the start screen, **New score** makes a blank score to build up part by part (with a score already open: **Edit → More → New score**). Choose a title, then add the lines you want with **Add a line**: give each line a name (rename it as you like) and a clef (treble, an octave lower, or bass). Then the key, the time signature, the speed and the number of bars, and **Create**. It opens straight in **Edit** with the first rest picked. Build it layer by layer: pick a rest in one line, put notes in on the piano or with **Sing it in**, then pick the next line's rest and do the same. Everything is kept quietly on this device as you go: if you close the app, the start screen offers **Continue “Your title”** next time. Several unsaved new scores are all kept: the newest is offered first, and the others are under **N more** below it. **Save a copy** makes `Your title.mscz`, which opens in MuseScore. The key, time signature, bars and lines can all be changed later with **More** in the Edit bar (below).

### Change the notes (Edit)

> **Not in the published app yet** (see the note under *Start a new score*).

**Edit** (the music-note button at the top) lets you change the music itself. This is new and still being tried out.

**Your own notes follow the music.** What you wrote or drew on the score with **Notes** stays with its bar when you edit: bars added before it carry it along, and a line added above moves it down with its staff. Deleting a bar takes the notes written on it too, and Undo brings them back with the bar. Clearing the music out of a bar (the bin, Cut) leaves your notes alone. A note in the margin or by the title stays where it is on the page. After editing, **Save** lights up when there are notes on the score, because they have moved with the music: save them together with a copy of the edited score, since the notes are kept for the score as it is now.

1. Tap **Edit**. A bar appears under the top buttons, and at the bottom, over the playing controls, the note lengths with a **piano** under them.
2. Tap a note or rest on the score. It gets a blue outline, and the bar says what it is, for example *C♯5 · quarter · bar 2*. A note sounds when you pick it.
3. Change it:
   - the arrows **‹ ›** go to the previous or next note;
   - **⌄ ⌃** move the note down or up a step; turn on **Octave** to move by an octave instead;
   - the note shapes (whole to 32nd) set how long it is, and the dot makes it half as long again;
   - the bin turns a note into a rest.
   - **Put notes in on the piano.** Pick a note or rest, then tap a key: its note goes in there and the next place is picked, so a tune goes in key by key, as with MuseScore's piano keyboard. Slide the piano sideways for higher or lower keys (every white key has its name; middle C is **C4**). The key of the picked note is lit, and when you pick a note the piano moves to it. A note goes in as long as the note shape chosen: on a rest, or while you are putting notes in, the shapes set the length of the notes about to go in (a quarter to start). The **rest key** beside the shapes puts in a rest of that length. A note that is longer than the room left in its bar is tied over the barline, as in MuseScore. A key tapped on existing music replaces it. To fix a note, tap **‹** to go back to it, use **⌄ ⌃** (or **Octave**), then **›** to carry on.
   - **Chords:** turn on **Chord** (beside the rest key) and the next keys join the note you just put in instead of going in after it; with a note picked, they join that note. All the keys of a picked chord are lit. Turn **Chord** off to carry on with the tune.
   - **One line at a time.** On a phone, a score with several lines opens in Edit showing one line by itself (the picked note's, or the first), so the notes are big enough to tap. The **lines button** (three sliders, at the top right of the bar) lists the lines and **All lines**; tablets and computers start on all lines and have the same button. It only changes what you see while editing: every line still plays, a saved copy has every line, and **Done** brings them all back.
   - **Several notes at once (a range):** pick a note, then **hold your finger on another** for a moment (with a mouse: Shift+click). Everything between them is selected, on every line in between, and the bar shows *Bars 3–5 · 2 lines selected*. Then **Copy** or **Cut** (they take the place of **‹ ›**), **⌄ ⌃** to move it all up or down, or the bin to clear it. After copying, **Paste** appears at the end of the note shapes: pick where it should start and tap it. What you copy stays ready to paste into another score too. A plain tap ends the range.
4. **Sing it in** (the microphone button in the bar) turns a tune you sing or hum into notes. First pick the note or rest where the tune should start: the box names the line and the bar it will go into, and a tune longer than the bar carries on into the next bars. Then **Record** (or **Choose a recording**), sing one tune, one note at a time, and tap **Stop**. While you record, a **Metronome** beeps the beat so you can sing in time, at the **speed** of the bar you picked (120 beats a minute where the score gives none); change the speed before recording if you like, or switch the metronome off. The first note you sing starts the tune, so there is no need to wait for a particular beep. With earphones the beeps stay out of the recording; without them PocketScore filters them out. PocketScore finds the notes on your device (nothing is uploaded) and draws them. Check them with **Listen**, change the **speed** or the **shortest note** if they look wrong, use **Octave** to move them, then **Put in the score**. They go in from the picked note on and replace what is there; **Undo** takes the whole tune out in one step. This is new: it works best with a clear voice, one note at a time, and it can get notes wrong, so always listen back. Only one voice at a time for now.
5. **More** (the three dots in the bar) opens the less-used tools:
   - **Sing it in** (the microphone) is here too;
   - **♯ ♭ ♮** change the picked note's accidental, **Tie** joins it to the next note of the same height, **Triplet** turns it into three in the time of two;
   - **Voice 1 2 3 4** puts two or more tunes on one line (soprano and alto on one staff, say). On a note, it moves the note to that voice. On a rest, while putting notes in, it starts that voice at the same place, so the next keys go into it;
   - **Marks**: staccato, accent, tenuto and marcato on the picked note or every note of a range (tap again to take one off), a fermata, a breath mark or a caesura;
   - **Phrasing**: a **Slur** over a range (or from the picked note to the next), a **cresc.** or **dim.** hairpin under it;
   - **Dynamics** (ppp to fff) goes under the picked note, **Words → Above** puts text above it, and **Lyric** puts a syllable under it (end it with **-** when the word goes on) and picks the next note so you can type on;
   - **From this bar**, **Key** and **Time** change the key or the time signature from the picked bar on, on every line;
   - **Bars**: type how many, then **Before this bar** (empty bars before the picked one) or **At the end**; **Delete bar** takes the picked bar (or the bars of a range) out of every line, after asking;
   - **Lines**: type a name and **Rename** the picked note's line, or choose a clef and **Add a line** at the bottom (it plays with the same sound as the line above; its sound can be changed in the Mixer);
   - **Score → New score** starts another score from a blank page without going back to the start screen (the same as **New score** there, above). If this score has changes, PocketScore first asks whether to keep them.
   Adding or taking away a line reloads the sounds for a moment, and sounds you picked for lines in the Mixer go back to the score's own.
6. **Undo** and **Redo** are at the top right of the bar. **Done** closes the bar and the piano.
7. **With a keyboard** (a computer, or an iPad with one), MuseScore's keys work: **A–G** put notes in (in the key of the piece, in the octave nearest the note before), **0** a rest, **5** quarter, **4** eighth, **3** 16th, **6** half, **7** whole, **.** a dot, **← →** move, **Shift+← →** make a range, **↑ ↓** move a note up or down (**Ctrl+↑ ↓** an octave), **Delete**, **Ctrl+C / X / V**, **Ctrl+Z**, **Ctrl+Y**.

**Save a copy** (the download arrow) makes a new `.mscz` file called *Your score (edited).mscz*. It opens in MuseScore or in PocketScore. **It never replaces the file you opened.** If you open another score or start a new one first, PocketScore asks whether to save a copy, as MuseScore does when a score is closed (a new score counts as unsaved even before anything is put in). **Don't keep** throws a new score away for good, since it has no file behind it.

Good to know: until you save a copy, your changes are kept quietly on this device. If the app closes, opening the same score again asks whether to **Continue my edits** or **Start again** from the file as it is. The pen (**Notes**) is for marking up a score you are reading, so it is a separate mode: Notes and Edit are never on together (opening one closes the other), and drawings stay where they were drawn and may not line up once the music is changed. To write something on the score as a composer would, use **More → Words** (real text in the score).

### Save
**Save** (top bar) keeps your notes and mixer changes for this score, on this device.

- It turns blue when there's something to save.
- Opening another score with unsaved changes asks: **Save**, **Don't save** or **Cancel**.
- If the app closes before you save, nothing is lost: the changes come back next time, still unsaved.
- Your `.mscz` file is never changed.

<details>
<summary>More about saving</summary>

- The score is recognised by its contents: a renamed copy shows your notes too, but a version you
  edited and re-saved in MuseScore counts as a different score.
- On a keyboard, **Ctrl+S** (or **⌘S**) saves.
- If the device's storage is full, PocketScore tells you the changes could not be saved.

</details>

### Settings
Tap the **gear** at the top. On a phone, Settings opens as a page of its own; **‹ Back** (or your
phone's Back) returns to the score. Each setting works straight away and is kept on this device, for
every score. Until you change them, PocketScore works as described above.

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Keep playing when I leave PocketScore** | The music goes on in another app, on the home screen or with the screen locked, for listening on the go. Off: it pauses. On Android, PocketScore then shows in your notifications while it plays, with play and pause there. On iPhone and iPad the system may still stop it |
| **Metronome on when a score opens** | Every score opens with the click on. Off: it starts muted |
| **Tap a note to hear it** | While stopped, a tap on a note plays it. Off: the tap only moves to that note |
| **Start focus by itself** | Hides the bars when you leave the screen alone |
| **Wait while playing** / **while stopped** | How long before focus starts by itself (4 and 10 seconds at first) |
| **Full screen with the Focus button** | The phone's or browser's own bars go too. Not on iPhone, which doesn't allow it |
| **Open scores in** | The view every score opens in: *Page view*, *Page by page*, *Two pages* or *Continuous* |
| **Two pages: a swipe turns both** | In *Two pages*, each swipe shows the next two pages (1-2, then 3-4). Off: it moves on one page, the right page going to the left (1-2, then 2-3) |
| **Follow the music** | The score moves along as it plays. Off: it stays where you put it |
| **Keep the screen on while playing** | The screen doesn't dim or lock in the middle of a piece |
| **Playback report**, *Send* | If the music stutters or lags, tap **Send** soon after. Your phone's share menu opens, and the report is also copied, so you can paste it into a [GitHub issue](https://github.com/HowieYHY/mobilemusescore/issues) or a message. It tells how the device kept up during your recent plays: times, score names and the device's own figures, never your notes or files. It works offline and later too: the log stays on the device after the app is closed. Please add what you were doing and whether battery saver was on or the phone was warm, which apps can't see |

## Questions

<details>
<summary><b>Does it sound like MuseScore on my computer?</b></summary>

Yes, with MuseScore's standard **MS Basic** sounds: PocketScore uses the same sound engine and sounds
as MuseScore 4.7.5. In side-by-side tests the volume, timing and tone matched desktop MuseScore.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>What about Muse Sounds or other sound libraries?</b></summary>

Those only work in desktop MuseScore. Parts that use them play with the nearest MS Basic sound,
and the mixer tells you which ones.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Does the page look exactly like MuseScore?</b></summary>

Very nearly. PocketScore lays scores out with MuseScore's own code, so pages and page breaks
match. Spacing can differ very slightly, because phones measure text a little differently.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Can I edit scores?</b></summary>

No. PocketScore is for reading, playing and practising. Make changes in MuseScore.

</details>

## Something not working?

| Problem | Try this |
| --- | --- |
| No sound on iPhone / iPad | Check the volume buttons, and that Bluetooth isn't playing the sound elsewhere |
| "Audio could not start" | Close PocketScore completely, reopen it, tap Play |
| Drawing scrolls the page (or it won't scroll) | Check **Draw with finger** in the notes bar |
| Choppy sound | Check you have the latest version (start screen). PocketScore also adjusts itself: if the sound stutters, it prepares more sound ahead and tells you once. If it keeps happening, send a playback report (**Settings** > **Playback report** > **Send**) soon after, even days later is fine |
| A score won't open | It must be a `.mscz` file saved by MuseScore |
| The music stops a few minutes after leaving the app, with **Keep playing** on (Android) | A known problem, being fixed ([#28](https://github.com/HowieYHY/mobilemusescore/issues/28)). Until then, keep PocketScore on screen while you listen |
| A light grey strip under the bottom bar (Android, installed before version 1.0.17) | It's only cosmetic. Android paints it with the colours the app had when it was installed. Chrome is meant to update them by itself, but it can take days or never happen. To hurry it, go to `chrome://webapks` in Chrome, find PocketScore, tap **Request update**, then swipe PocketScore away and open it again. If the strip is still there, reinstall. First open the PocketScore address in a normal Chrome tab and check your notes and settings show up (then they are kept). Then remove the app from the home screen without deleting its data, and add it again from Chrome |
| A light strip under the bottom bar (iPhone or iPad, added to the home screen before version 1.0.3) | It's only cosmetic. To remove it, open PocketScore in Safari or Chrome until the start screen says 1.0.3 or later, then add it to your home screen again. **The new icon starts without your notes and settings** (each icon keeps its own), so keep the old one until you no longer need them |

**Report a problem:** [open an issue](https://github.com/HowieYHY/mobilemusescore/issues) or message
the developer. Say which device and system version, which score, what you did and what happened.
A screenshot or screen recording helps a lot.
